Say g'day to the unexpected in Australia Everyone knows Australia has spectacular beaches and kangaroos-but it also has unexpected and memorable surprises for travellers in the know.

There’s a reason why Canadians who have visited Australia rave about the country, some returning time and time again.

And we’re going to let you in on a secret: It’s not just because the country has great beaches and beautiful weather (although that doesn’t hurt). It’s because Australia is capable of delivering surprises that you don’t often see in marketing brochures.

If you’re thinking about booking your first trip to Australia, here’s an insider’s guide on what you need to know about the country.

Pack your stretchy pants.

Imagine this: You’re in a country where nearly every kind of produce can be grown with relative ease—from Queensland’s juicy mangoes, to South Australia’s stonefruit, to the Australian Capital Territory’s truffles. Then, add to the mix internationally renowned wine regions like South Australia’s Barossa, Western Australia’s Margaret River region, and Canberra’s Wine District, alongside countless distilleries and craft breweries. Garnish with some award-winning chefs and you’ll end up with a country that serves up culinary experiences rivalling the greats.

Your average pub in Australia serves up fresh flavours, but this is the place to splash out. Making a booking at one of Australia’s “hatted” restaurants (the country’s answer to Michelin stars) is a must; it’s just as much about the experience as it is about the food, with incomparable dining locations. Take, for example, Bennelong in Sydney/Warrane, where you dine inside the sails of the Opera House while gazing out at the harbour. Then there’s Faro, inside Hobart/Nipaluna’s subversive Mona Museum, where you’ll tuck in directly next to a James Turrell light installation.

Wherever you eat, keep an eye out for native ingredients like lemon myrtle, saltbush, crocodile and Davidson’s plum on the menu. Just don’t offend a local by asking them to put “shrimp on the barbie.” They’re called prawns here.

Arrive without expectations.

Research all you want; Australia is a country with more than a few surprises up its sleeves.

Case in point: Everyone knows Australia has spectacular beaches, the Great Barrier Reef, the Red Centre and the Great Ocean Road. But did you know that it also has a mountain range that runs the entire length of the country, which is snow-capped in the south and covered in tropical rainforest in the north?

Then, there are the animals. Everyone wants to see koalas and kangaroos. But there are also hundreds of unusual endemic species you’ve likely never heard of, including tree kangaroos, pademelons, wallaroos, bandicoots, gliders, quokkas, quolls, bettongs, dunnarts, potoroos—and that’s just the marsupials. There are also native lizards, birds, fish, plants and more.

Australia’s Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures also offer incredible and surprising diversity. Representing 65,000 years of living history, there are hundreds of language groups (tribes) spread across the country, each with their own unique traditions and cultures. Only some use boomerangs, play the didgeridoo and make dot paintings. Fortunately, Indigenous tour operators across the country—from Walkabout Adventures in Tropical North Queensland to Dreamtime Southern Cross in Sydney, or Koomal Dreaming in Western Australia—give valuable insight into their strong connection to the land.

Finally, Australia is closer than you think. With airlines including Air Canada offering direct service between Canada and Sydney/Warrane, Melbourne/Narrm and Brisbane/Meeanjin, you’re only a few movies, a nap, and a meal service away.

“G’day” isn’t just ‘an expression—it’s a way of life.

There’s a reason why Canadians and Australians are known to have an affinity for one another, and it’s not just our shared sense of humour. It’s more likely due to our shared welcoming spirit and day-to-earth attitudes, which is why “g’day” is an expression you’ll come to hear daily.

Australian locals are eager to show you their country, highlighting the qualities that make it unlike anywhere else in the world. Soon, you’ll realize that the invitation to come and say g’day is truly genuine—and it won’t be long until you’re saying g’day back.
