By focusing on the importance of traditional culture,

CINUP provides group insurance that helps Indigenous communities continue their traditional way of life.
2

For Indigenous communities in Canada, ensuring that business deals are based on respect can make a large difference in the very health of the community members. Ensuring sensitivity to the specific cultural needs is a necessity for insurance providers who want the best for their clients.

Winnipeg-based CINUP provides group insurance to approximately 400 First Nations and Inuit communities and businesses across Canada. For CINUP Vice-President Karen Vince, working with First Nations and Inuit partners means ensuring their voices are heard, and that collaboration forms the foundation of the working relationship between CINUP and it's stakeholders. To accomplish that, CINUP offers specific products with a focus on health and wellness. Their program coverage includes hunting and fishing activities, a lifestyle spending account which includes items such as ceremonial tobacco, spiritual advisor, elder counselling, and travel coverage in Canada for medical purposes—an incredibly important tool for those who live in areas with fewer resources.

“A lot of our communities are remote,” says Vince, “and these programs are very important to those who need to travel for medical reasons.”

“We know that gaining trust and showing our integrity through partnership is what is most important.”

This, of course, is the result of having spent time with clients and learning firsthand about their needs. “Having a good understanding of their community, and meeting face to face, whether that means the chief and council, the elders, or plan members who have our program,” says Vince. “Participating in some of their events, whether it’s a powwow, donating sponsorship monies, it’s important for us to give back.”

“It’s really important we understand where they come from and what they’ve gone through. Sales pitches aren’t important to our clients. We know that gaining trust and showing that we want to work in partnership through collaboration is what is most important.”

To ensure CINUP is able to meet the specific needs of the communities with which they partner, they needed to ensure that their own insurance provider was equally dedicated to serving the needs of those communities. “Desjardins is very open to helping us be creative,” says Vince.

“They know that we know our clients best, so they work with us to make sure we can meet our clients’ needs.”

Desjardins Insurance has supported CINUP as they have created new initiatives and programs. One community in particular presented a challenge not being met by other insurance companies. This, says Vince, necessitated the creation of a brand-new program. “The community wanted to give back to all of their members, not only those who were part of a particular business, but every single member. We built this program with them based on that need.” Vince says that now, a half-dozen other members run a similar program.

“A program that our clients have been asking for will be introduced shortly, EHN Intensive out-patient program. A one-year program that will provide virtual individual and group counselling sessions along with a personalized recovery plan for members who are struggling with mental health challenges, including addiction, substance abuse, depression, anxiety and much more.”

Other benefits also include dependent eligibility for children adopted by custom—a service not offered by many standard insurers. “Your typical group insurance plan only allows for biological dependents or situations of legal guardianship,” says Vince. “Our plan recognizes that many community members help to raise children outside of these typical specific boundaries.

Programs like this are a lot of work, but for Vince, the extra effort is worth it.

“It’s not about the the time and effort , it’s about the goodwill to the community.”
