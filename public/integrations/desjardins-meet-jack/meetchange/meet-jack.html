Everyone wants to envision their environment as a safe and inclusive space, where people can work, learn, and grow together. It’s important to ensure that meaningful practices and policies are developed and put in place so that all employees feel welcomed, accepted and supported.

It's not always easy to know the best approach to adopt in creating safe spaces for people in the 2SLGBTQ+ community. This is, however, a task undertaken together, fostering an environment where they can thrive alongside their fellow workers.

A year-round commitment to impactful change

“Looking at progressive policies should happen all year round.” says Jack Saddleback, an international public speaker living in Saskatoon. ”Webinars or trainings should happen outside of June.

Trans and non-binary Canadians are more likely to experience inappropriate behaviours in public, online and at work than their cisgender counterparts. Seeing these changes in the workplace can facilitate a monumental impact in the lives of trans and non-binary employees.

Committing to education

Look for guidance in progressive policies and seek training from organizations that are striving to make big strides in the support and care of 2SLGBTQ+ communities. Understand as well that policy changes over time and that the current reality for trans and non-binary people continually evolves. Better education ensures that trans and non-binary employees are better supported in the present and are able to feel safe as time and policies change.

Make sure your employees feel safe speaking up

One way to lessen the burden on trans and non-binary employees is ensuring protected avenues for them to speak up if and when they are put into a position where they need to. In order to include all employees, it is incumbent on those in a position of power to keep doors open to them. Letting employees speak freely about their experience in the workplace without fear of retribution creates a necessary path to constructive and open dialogue.

Work with your trans and non-binary employees to create safe practices

Work with your team to understand what their needs are in the workplace and develop best practices together to ensure their work experience is safe for them at all times. Create a series of checks and balances then meet regularly to ensure that goals are established and actionable steps are being delivered upon to ensure success.

Adopting an intersectional approach

“When we take concrete movements going forward, it doesn’t just help the 2SLGBT+ community, it helps everyone. It helps us to recognize the humanity in each other,” Saddleback says. Creating policies and year-round commitments to education, dialogue and support ensure that employees across intersectional lines are supported with individual needs that may differ from community to community. This allows for deeper conversations on the unique challenges faced by a diverse array of people, “doing something for pride month leads us into conversation about Black and Indigenous people of color, and some of the challenges faced by Black and Indigenous people of colour that are also 2SLGBTQ+.”

Let actions do more work than words

Many trans and non-binary people have faced challenges in the workplace, even in those that pride themselves on being more progressive. There can often be subtle microaggressions that make people feel othered. Saddleback recalls a former supervisor telling him that “men don’t act this way” or compelling him to “man up” as examples of the ways that gender can at times be imposed on individuals. Lead with compassion, letting individuals live and love as they are and support them as such. There are no rules to gender, and by abolishing gendered expectations all employees can live authentically.

Encouraging good practices that make trans and non-binary employees feel included

Work together to normalize best practices, such as encouraging employees to add their pronouns to email signatures, and adopt gender inclusive language in communications that are internal and external to the company. Make sure trans people feel safe using washrooms and public use spaces. These are small, simple steps that can go a long way in ensuring the workplace is safe, inclusive, and inviting to all employees now and in the future.

Be aware that this is a year-round commitment

Creating lasting support for 2SLGBTQ+ employees doesn’t begin and end with the month of June, rather it is a deliberate decision made year-round to work with and for 2SLGBTQ+ employees in making sure they are supported. Committing to outreach as a regular practice and reviewing your annual programming plan can ensure that trans and non-binary employees are uplifted, as Saddleback says, “365 days of the year”.

Leave the door open for change

Be aware that gender identity is a malleable and fluid idea, and that employees may or may not live in a static identity forever. The groundwork you lay now can prove to be fruitful soil for employees of all identities to thrive and survive.

Building better workplaces necessitates a deep commitment to putting in the work that gets you there. Where people are given the space to not just survive but thrive and flourish. “Regardless of whatever identity you may have, you deserve love, you deserve caring,” Saddleback says,
