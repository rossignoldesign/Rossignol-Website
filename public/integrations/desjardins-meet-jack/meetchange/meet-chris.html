Watch how Chris Bergeron is advocating for inclusivity in the workplace and how Desjardins Insurance is helping companies live their values

Work-life balance has been an ongoing topic of discussion for many employees, and the last few years have illustrated how essential it is for employees to not only have that balance, but to also have the support of their employers in achieving it. For some, this means flexible work-from-home schedules, mental-health days or top-ups on a standard parental leave offering. But for almost all, it certainly means that the places they choose to work share values of diversity and inclusivity—and that the way a company approaches these values is more than just lip service.

“Today, a lot of businesses have the intention of doing better,” says Chris Bergeron, an author and speaker, vice president of creative inclusivity for a major Canadian marketing and communications agency and member of the trans community. This is thanks in large part to the push from employees, but employers are benefiting too—valuing equality in the workplace minimizes the risk of missing out on top talent. “You want to find out whether a place is a good place to work, look at the way they treat people from minority groups,” says Bergeron. “Do they have programs in place in order to affect the balance of things—do they care?” Asking these questions in the hiring process is what has shaped the incredible change in business that Bergeron says she has witnessed, especially in the last five years. This change is allowing more and more people to seek out roles they’re excited about without fear of settling for good enough when it comes to workplace support and treatment. So, how can employers ensure that not only are they meeting their employees’ expectations and needs, but that they are also doing the real work to combat unconscious bias and promote inclusivity in the workplace?

Opt for comprehensive and inclusive healthcare packages

While in Canada we’re lucky to have provincial healthcare coverage, it often falls short in areas that can have the greatest impact on equity-deserving groups. But this situation also presents an opportunity for employers to reimagine the scope of their group insurance plans. By choosing to offer less conventional benefits such as parental leave and mental health and gender affirmation coverage, they can help level the playing field for employees with unique needs.

Gender affirmation coverage is a particularly potent example of a benefit with an outsized impact. Martin Nadon, director of product development and training at Desjardins Insurance, explains the value it offers. “Our clients [employers] play an important role in supporting employees who want to transition, and we are an extension of our clients. Being close to our clients, and sharing those values of inclusivity, diversity and equality in our own organization… it really was a matter of making sure we would be there to support individuals so that they can have the quality of life that they want, and they expect.”

Comprehensive healthcare that provides coverage for a wider range of surgeries related to transitioning is just one way that a company can show up for their employees in a way that makes a huge difference—which is why gender affirmation coverage is part of the latest offering from Desjardins Insurance.

Provide education and training to leaders

The education component is a crucial one, and companies can benefit from partnering with experts to assist with this element. Organizations with a focus on diversity, especially workplace diversity, are a good place to start. One of these is Fondation Émergence, a non-profit organization dedicated to fighting homophobia and transphobia, with a workplace program called ProAlly. Among their initiatives to promote inclusivity and provide education, they have developed guides and tools to help businesses become more equitable, while attracting talent that prioritizes diversity, equity and inclusion. Providing these resources at all levels of a company—and specifically senior levels of management—helps to broaden understanding of the context and differences that play an important role in the lives of their employees. Desjardins Insurance chose to partner with the Fondation Émergence to develop a gender affirmation toolkit for employers that offer this type of coverage. “We said at the outset that we are not the experts,” says Nadon. “We really wanted to work with an organization to ensure we were going in the right direction and to have this ongoing conversation with the Fondation Émergence and make sure that we would get the right level of information and context so that our offering would meet the most needs as possible.” The result isn’t just a robust benefit plan, but also tools and resources to raise awareness and share information about a range of related topics and questions.

Implement predetermined performance evaluations

Unconscious bias can and does unfairly single out minority groups. One way to prevent this type of discrimination is to make sure that transparent performance metrics are in place. This will help ensure that everyone—from entry-level employees to CEOs—are aware of job requirements and how success is measured. Equally important is to make sure these metrics are communicated openly and to everyone. There should be no surprises when it comes to job feedback. Even more important, no one should ever have to wonder if their sex, gender expression or race has anything to do with the professional feedback they receive. As Bergeron notes, everybody should get to show up and be ambitious at work, without fear or judgement based on their differences.

Speaking about hiring trans people, and indeed all minorities, Bergeron notes that the idea of minorities being fragile is misguided.
